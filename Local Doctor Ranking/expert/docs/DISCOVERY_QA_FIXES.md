# Discovery QA corrections

This release follows live testing of the expert preview. It prioritises reliable, source-backed discovery. Optional generated explanations remain experimental; no new explanation success is claimed.

## Reproduced failures and correction scope

1. **Negative roles and preferred research.** The exact refinement “Only general practitioners for the clinical perspective; hospital dermatologists should not be included. Keep dermoscopy and primary care essential, and diagnostic research preferred” previously created an essential dermatologist role and essential research. GP aliases and explicit role polarity were absent, and importance parsing skipped a modifier that belonged to its matched phrase. The correction separates inclusion, exclusion and role-only restrictions, preserves explicit importance, and prevents a model patch from contradicting those local instructions. Role restrictions rely on attributable professional-role evidence; missing GP leads are not padded with dermatologists.
2. **Explicit adult cardiology missed.** Nabeel Ahmed's stored professional record states that he is a general adult cardiologist and reports CT coronary angiograms. The former evidence could not support Adults because the population and literal modality were not in the same clause. The correction relates explicit population practice to clinical activity in the same attributable source and clinical domain, with negative and conflicting-population guards. It does not infer adults from a generic cardiology title or infer current practice from a retrieval date.
3. **New assessment contaminated by the old one.** Home and refinement used the same session, draft and filters. A skin-lesion request submitted from Home retained cardiac CT, coronary disease and Adults. New assessment and resume/refine now have distinct intent; an existing project's evidence, notes and history must survive starting a separate assessment.
4. **Evidence hidden or truncated.** Comparison used the start of a biography or an attribution-only literal quote such as “Paul Norris”. Requirement-specific supporting spans now preserve the supporting clause, source identity, reviewed-summary label and review limitations. Historical study authorship remains distinct from diagnostic appraisal competence or primary-care practice.
5. **Narrow-screen usability.** At 320 × 740, the empty composer had 36px of visible height but 59px of content. Expanded brief and controls left little reading space, and the completion notice claimed a draft existed when it was empty. These behaviors require targeted sizing, disclosure and notice corrections, plus a readable comparison layout.
6. **Assessment questions became unsupported credentials.** A request for “UK clinicians who personally report cardiac CT” and can “explain how false positives and false negatives could affect patient management” could acquire an essential generic Clinician role or an essential activity/research requirement for the discussion itself through the model patch. The parser now rejects generic professional labels as occupations and keeps the requested discussion as engagement context. Personally reporting the requested imaging remains a clinical activity requirement; explicitly preferred diagnostic-study experience stays preferred. The boundary also preserves a separately requested, quoted experience requirement rather than discarding every phrase containing “explain”.
7. **Explicit essential activity was underweighted in ordering.** Personally reporting the requested cardiac CT could be supported in the evidence matrix yet contribute only to a secondary relevance score: the primary clinical score previously counted modality, condition and procedure anchors, excluding explicit activity. Documented essential activity now contributes to that primary clinical score after the matrix has scoped it to the requested modality. An interest in CT, reporting a different modality, optional activity and generic research/context do not receive that priority. This changes evidence-based ordering, not a claim of current practice or assessor qualification.

## Verification protocol

- Reproduce the original examples before changes; add nearby reversals, negation, modality and population counterexamples.
- Run the full expert/evaluation suite and patient regressions.
- Replay the frozen 14 development and 28 held-out briefs with actual BM25, actual embeddings and hybrid ranking. This is a regression replay, not a fresh independent evaluation.
- Run `evaluation/verify-discovery-regressions.cjs` against the complete local corpus and the hosted release. Reports retain each request, interpretation mode, active brief and candidate evidence outcomes in the private ignored cache. No optional explanation requests are made.
- Verify Home/new/refine, project preservation, drafts, strict filtering, comparison and mobile layouts in the browser. Keep physical-keyboard limitations separate from viewport checks.
- Deploy only the independent expert service, verify hosted asset hashes, health and patient-route isolation, then commit and push the expert branch.

## Verification results

- **624 expert/evaluation tests and 243 patient regressions pass (867 total).** No patient application or `synaptic_care_fe` files changed.
- The full corpus replay, round `discovery-fix-v3`, passed all **105 development and 228 held-out trust/refinement checks in each retrieval mode**. This reuses the frozen 14/28-brief evaluation packs; it is not a new independent assessment.
- Held-out known-reference recall at 20 was **95.833% hybrid, 93.750% BM25-only and 91.667% semantic-only**. Central-requirement source-supported precision at five was 100% in the evaluated answerable cases. These are narrow reviewed-reference metrics, not exhaustive corpus recall or clinical superiority.
- The unchanged corpus is `expert-corpus-v1-fc67aacfcc3999fc230c`: 40,876 source rows, 40,221 identity groups, 22,271 indexed candidates and 273,108 passages. Only four candidates have the additional public-source checks described in the original audit. No database rows were rewritten.
- The final model-merge correction also collapses identical kind/text requirements into the existing deterministic ID. Five regressions cover duplicate questions, whitespace/case, independent questions, removal and importance updates. The final parser produces identical deterministic briefs across all 42 frozen cases/64 turns, verified by `verify-brief-equivalence.cjs`; the retrieval implementation did not change after the full replay.
- The exact nine-request workflow passed **37/37 locally** and **37/37 hosted** before the final duplicate-criterion check was added. The hosted cardiac example ranked the explicit CT reporter first, retained him under documented essentials, and preserved that result through adult removal/readdition. All seven requests eligible for model interpretation used DeepSeek in that hosted run. GP-only refinement returned two attributable role matches, without padding with dermatologists; later replacement and clearing broadened the population correctly.

### Browser observations

Hosted checks covered desktop, tablet and 390px layouts; 320px checks covered comparison, empty composer sizing and multiline growth. Card opening with Enter and closing with Escape restored focus. A new draft typed while the first request was pending survived response completion with focus retained and no page scroll. Strict filtering reduced the cardiac shortlist to one candidate without moving or clearing the composer. Starting a skin assessment from Home dropped the old cardiac requirements while the prior saved candidate and draft remained available in its project. The comparison retained the dated primary-care study summary and its coauthorship limitations. Optional AI was collapsed and labelled experimental. No warning/error console entries appeared in the final hosted browser inspection.

An eight-line 320px draft capped at 140px and scrolled internally; the empty composer no longer overflowed. These are browser viewport checks, not physical mobile-keyboard certification. Reduced-motion behavior is covered by the existing CSS/interaction regressions; it is not a new physical-device observation.

### Preserved unsuccessful observations

The first hosted regression round was 34/36: a model-created engagement-question credential emptied the strict shortlist. That result was retained and the parser was corrected. After the activity-priority fix, a newly added assertion incorrectly searched the display label for “report”, although the canonical label is “Image interpretation”. The 36/37 report is retained; inspection confirmed the first candidate had documented support. The assertion now follows the quoted requirement ID, and the subsequent 37/37 run passed. No failed report was overwritten.

One local UI request timed out while the same process was running the full offline retrieval evaluation. Its draft and error state survived. Interactive latency is therefore measured separately on the hosted service without a concurrent evaluation or browser search.

### Final hosted acceptance and performance

The optimized release passed **38/38 hosted workflow checks**, including duplicate-criterion prevention, and **11/11 route/asset/isolation checks**. Twenty sequential warm searches returned sourced results: **median 3,094 ms, p95 5,139 ms**, meeting the eight-second target for this run. All twenty responses carried DeepSeek-backed interpretation; cached interpretations are included and this does not mean twenty new model calls.

The earlier twenty-search run is preserved: median 7,338 ms, p95 11,153 ms, 20/20 results, with eight DeepSeek interpretations and twelve explicit fallbacks. Investigation found repeated segmentation and classification of the same professional passages. A passage-keyed WeakMap now caches only source clause/type descriptors, invalidating them when relevant source content changes. Query-specific matching, evidence ownership, qualifications and ranking still run per brief. Nine cache regressions cover source changes and evidence scope. The model and six-second interpretation budget were unchanged. Upstream model responsiveness also varied between runs, so the complete hosted improvement cannot be attributed solely to caching.

The same-index offline comparison completed at 21:38:29 UTC: **42/42 complete hybrid response objects were identical** before and after the cache change, including ranks, matrix, evidence and diagnostics. It used one full real-data index, alternated implementation order and made no database, model-API or HTTP requests. Twenty pairs without new query embeddings had baseline median 1,852 ms/p95 5,828 ms versus optimized median 959 ms/p95 2,280 ms on the local Windows machine. Twenty-two embedding-affected pairs were excluded from that warm comparison. These are local retrieval timings, separate from hosted end-to-end latency. The immutable `search-performance-discovery-cache.json` report records source hashes and all 84 calls; `verify-search-performance.cjs` reproduces the method using a supplied private baseline source file.

One acceptance attempt began a second before the index became Ready and received 503; another readiness request timed out while Railway was switching traffic. Neither is counted as a completed warm run. The workflow harness now checks readiness before beginning. Final deployment, asset and latency observations are recorded in [deployment verification](DEPLOYMENT_VERIFICATION.md). Optional generated explanations remain deferred and are not counted as repaired or newly validated by this release. The sourced discovery and comparison path does not require them.
